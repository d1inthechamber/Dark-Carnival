# Dark Carnival — Visual Bible

## Core identity

Dark Carnival should feel like a cursed 1990s supernatural horror comic that has come alive, not a generic dark fantasy UI and not a collection of unrelated AI illustrations.

The visual recipe is:
- heavy black inks and deep shadow shapes
- scratchy cross-hatching, distressed print texture, dirty paper edges
- exaggerated perspective and strong silhouettes
- selective neon carnival color against mostly dark values
- comic-panel composition rather than flat menu screens
- snappy, high-energy 2D animation with impact frames and bold posing
- grotesque humor alongside horror
- readable game UI at all times

## Anti-"AI slop" rules

Avoid:
- symmetrical character-center compositions on every screen
- generic purple/blue fantasy lighting everywhere
- excessive glowing smoke, random particles, ornamental clutter
- smooth plastic rendering with no ink texture
- interchangeable demonic clowns that do not belong to this universe
- fake text, fake logos, extra fingers, meaningless props
- using the same camera angle repeatedly

Prefer:
- unusual panel crops
- strong foreground silhouettes
- asymmetric framing
- purposeful props tied to the current encounter
- limited palettes per location
- recurring visual motifs that players can learn
- visible hand-drawn imperfections

## Neon palette philosophy

Neon is punctuation, not wallpaper.

Primary accent families:
- toxic green
- hot magenta
- electric cyan
- radioactive yellow
- blood red
- occasional ultraviolet purple

Keep backgrounds mostly black, charcoal, dirty cream, rust, or bruised gray so neon interactions remain readable.

## Animation target

Use the broad qualities of premium modern 2D comic animation:
- strong anticipation and follow-through
- exaggerated poses
- hard impact frames
- dramatic camera punches
- limited animation for dialogue, fuller motion for key events
- animated lighting and foreground layers
- cards that physically slam, flip, tear, burn, spray, or distort

Do not imitate another production's exact character designs, frames, or proprietary visual assets.

## Recurring universe rules

- The user-supplied Hatchet Man reference is the authoritative mascot/logo asset wherever the mascot appears.
- Shaggy 2 Dope must remain bald and follow the supplied face-paint reference.
- Violent J, Jamie Madrox, and Monoxide Child should follow the supplied reference mapping rather than earlier generated labels.
- Faygo is a meaningful resource and visual motif, not a background Easter egg.
- The Carnival should visually remember choices: props, stains, damaged cards, repeated NPC details, altered signs, and callback imagery can reappear later.

## Chapter color identities

### Carnival of Carnage
Filthy red, toxic green, dirty yellow, black. Chaotic urban midway.

### Ringmaster
Crimson, gold, black. Oppressive big-top geometry and judgment imagery.

### Riddle Box
Cyan, magenta, sick green. Warped funhouse optics, impossible perspective.

### The Great Milenko
Ultraviolet, acid green, theatrical gold. Illusion, temptation, false beauty.

### The Amazing Jeckel Brothers
Fire orange, white-hot yellow, black, red. Motion, juggling, consequence.

### The Wraith
Restrained palette. Near-monochrome judgment that branches toward luminous warmth or pit-like red-black horror.

## UI principles

- Major encounters read as comic pages/panels.
- Choice buttons feel integrated into the page rather than generic app controls.
- Controller focus must be unmistakable.
- HUD resources remain readable but visually secondary to the encounter art.
- Cards should eventually become illustrated physical objects with distinct silhouettes and animation.
- Text should never compete with essential art or disappear into distressed textures.
