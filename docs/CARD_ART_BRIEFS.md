# Dark Carnival — First Four Card Art Briefs

These four cards establish the visual grammar for every future Dark Carnival card. They should feel like cursed physical objects from the carnival itself, not generic fantasy cards and not clean mobile-game UI.

## Shared card system

### Format
- Master art: 1024×1536
- Vertical card silhouette with slightly imperfect, distressed edges
- Central illustration occupies roughly 60% of the face
- Title zone at top, effect zone at bottom
- Gameplay text should be rendered by Godot wherever practical
- Art should still read clearly when reduced to controller-menu size

### Shared construction
Every card should include:
- heavy black ink masses
- scratchy cross-hatching
- worn paper / printed-comic texture
- one dominant accent color plus one secondary accent
- a recurring carnival border language
- small visual evidence that the card is an object: bent corner, stain, tear, staple mark, grease-pencil notation, ticket punch, or rubbed edge
- a subtle recurring “Carnival remembers” motif, such as an eye-shaped notch, ticket punch, serial mark, or repeated sigil that can evolve later

Avoid:
- ornate fantasy filigree
- polished trading-card gradients
- glossy sci-fi frames
- random demonic faces
- fake baked-in text
- symmetric AI-poster compositions

## Card I — HATCHET

### Gameplay identity
Recover stolen cash. Violence rises.

### Emotional read
Immediate, aggressive, decisive. This should be the card that visually tells the player, “You can solve this fast, but the Carnival is recording how you solve it.”

### Illustration concept
A brutal close-up of a hatchet cutting diagonally through a carnival ticket, rope, or midway sign. The hatchet should feel physical and heavy rather than magical. Ink splatter and torn-paper debris can create motion without turning the image into gore.

### Composition
- strong diagonal from lower-left to upper-right
- hatchet head oversized by perspective
- torn ticket / snapped carnival rope / splintered booth wood behind it
- one small human silhouette or fleeing shadow can be present in the deep background, but the hatchet is the visual subject
- leave dark negative space behind the title zone

### Palette
- dominant: blood red
- secondary: dirty cream / radioactive yellow
- base: black, charcoal, rust
- tiny toxic-green accent allowed only as a carnival-light reflection

### Texture
- thick dry-brush blacks
- rough white scratch lines on the metal
- red misregistration around impact edges
- worn ink on border corners

### Focus state
When selected:
- red edge light ignites
- the card tilts slightly forward
- a thin white impact line flashes across the hatchet blade
- optional micro-jitter before confirm

### Play animation
1. card rises from hand
2. fast anticipation pause
3. card whips toward camera
4. one-frame red/black impact flash
5. diagonal slash crosses screen
6. cash recovery resolves
7. card snaps back/discards while a faint red stain remains on the card tray for a moment

### Sound/FX hook
Dry metal scrape → heavy paper/wood chop → short bass hit.

### Acceptance test
The card should feel dangerous before the player even reads its effect.

## Card II — FAYGO BREAK

### Gameplay identity
Spend 1 Faygo. Restore Resolve.

### Emotional read
Relief, humor, friendship, temporary sanity. It should feel genuinely refreshing but still belong inside the same dirty supernatural world.

### Illustration concept
A cold two-liter bottle exploding open under carnival lights, foam spraying upward like a celebratory fountain while the surrounding midway stays dark and grimy. The energy should be joyful without becoming a commercial-looking product shot.

### Composition
- low-angle bottle in lower third
- cap or spray arc leads the eye upward
- spray crosses the card frame slightly
- background can show distorted midway bulbs or silhouettes cheering / reaching
- a darker shadow shape behind the bottle keeps the image from feeling cute

### Palette
- dominant: electric cyan
- secondary: toxic green or hot magenta
- base: black, dirty violet, dead cream
- white highlights reserved for carbonation/spray

### Texture
- fizzy white stipple
- wet ink runs
- distressed halftone bubbles
- scuffed label area
- paper stain or ring mark near bottom border

### Focus state
When selected:
- cyan edge pulse
- animated bubbles travel upward
- very subtle vertical bob, as if carbonation is pushing the card up

### Play animation
1. card pops forward
2. cap shoots off-frame
3. cyan-white spray crosses foreground
4. UI briefly overexposes with fizz particles
5. Resolve meter pulses and settles
6. droplets linger for a beat on the card tray / panel frame

### Sound/FX hook
Cap crack → aggressive fizz → short crowd-like “whoop” texture or celebratory sting.

### Acceptance test
It should be the most playful card without breaking the horror-comic style.

## Card III — CARNIVAL SIGHT

### Gameplay identity
Lose a little Resolve to preview danger behind a choice.

### Emotional read
Forbidden knowledge. The card should feel like the Carnival is letting the player cheat fate—but charging them for looking.

### Illustration concept
A single enormous carnival eye opening inside a warped funhouse reflection. The pupil should contain a tiny preview-like scene fragment: a worker, a choice, a future consequence, or a distorted version of the player.

### Composition
- eye placed slightly off-center, never perfectly centered
- cracked-mirror geometry slices through the face of the card
- pupil acts as a “panel within the panel”
- frame edges can bend inward slightly to imply being watched

### Palette
- dominant: radioactive yellow-green
- secondary: electric cyan or magenta
- base: black, bruised gray, dirty violet

### Texture
- scratched iris lines
- mirrored halftone
- fingerprint smears
- black brush strokes that resemble eyelashes only at a glance
- faint ghost-image double print around the eye

### Focus state
When selected:
- pupil subtly tracks or expands
- green border glow tightens inward instead of spreading outward
- nearby text/UI can dim a touch so the eye feels dominant

### Play animation
1. card rotates slightly toward camera
2. eye opens
3. surrounding game screen desaturates
4. preview image flashes in pupil / screen panel
5. brief chromatic split or double-image
6. Resolve ticks down
7. world returns, but a faint afterimage remains for a moment

### Sound/FX hook
Paper creak → reversed carnival tone → soft camera-shutter / eyelid snap.

### Acceptance test
The player should feel uneasy about using it even though the information is useful.

## Card IV — BACK DOOR

### Gameplay identity
Escape the current encounter. Lose Resolve and honesty.

### Emotional read
Temptation, avoidance, trickery. It should look like a secret exit that probably should not exist.

### Illustration concept
A narrow painted door standing where no door belongs—perhaps between two carnival tents, inside a brick wall, or floating in black negative space. The door is cracked open just enough to show impossible cyan/magenta light beyond it.

### Composition
- door offset to one side
- perspective intentionally wrong by a few degrees
- long shadow points in a different direction than the visible light source
- footprints, ticket scraps, or clawed paint lead toward it
- one small “wrong” detail should reward close inspection, such as a doorknob on both sides or a shadow with no owner

### Palette
- dominant: ultraviolet purple
- secondary: electric cyan
- accent: hot magenta
- base: black, soot gray

### Texture
- peeling paint
- scraped wood grain
- black ink pooling in corners
- torn poster fragments around frame
- slight print-offset around the doorway

### Focus state
When selected:
- the dark gap in the door widens slightly
- cyan light leaks around the border
- card leans backward as though depth exists inside it

### Play animation
1. card slides forward
2. door on the card opens
3. the entire encounter panel warps toward the doorway
4. screen wipes through black
5. player reappears in the next location
6. Resolve drops
7. a small visual residue—door-shaped shadow, purple smear, or missing bit of border—lingers briefly

### Sound/FX hook
Old hinge → suction / reversed ambience → distant midway audio returning from the wrong direction.

### Acceptance test
It should feel useful, clever, and morally suspicious at the same time.

## Shared card back

The card back should eventually become as recognizable as the card faces.

### Direction
- matte black / dirty cream base
- distressed carnival ring or radial wheel motif
- space reserved for the authoritative user-supplied Hatchet Man reference once that source asset is available in the project
- no substitute or redrawn approximation of the Hatchet Man
- subtle neon registration marks
- scuffed ticket-number / serial-number feel

### Animation
When cards are dealt:
- backs should fan with slight individual rotation
- one or two should land imperfectly
- edge light should catch only during movement

---

## Card-hand presentation

The player should not feel like they opened a submenu. The hand should feel like a physical object layered over the current scene.

### Layout
- cards overlap slightly in widescreen mode
- focused card rises and moves forward
- controller navigation slides focus laterally with snap and small bounce
- non-focused cards remain readable but visually recede
- current encounter art remains visible behind the hand

### Controller feel
- D-pad / stick: move between cards
- A / Cross: play selected card
- B / Circle: lower hand
- LB / RB: quick cycle
- Y / Triangle: open/close hand where supported

### Motion rules
- quick, not floaty
- physical anticipation before slam
- impact frames are brief
- animation should never delay gameplay excessively
- reduced-motion fallback should preserve clarity without requiring the full effect

---

## Final card-fidelity checklist

Before these four cards are considered final-demo quality:
1. All four read as members of the same deck.
2. Each card is instantly identifiable by silhouette/palette.
3. None resemble generic fantasy TCG art.
4. Card art still works in mostly black-and-white inks.
5. Neon is used as emphasis, not full-surface glow.
6. Every card has an animation personality tied to its effect.
7. Controller focus is unmistakable.
8. Gameplay text remains readable at demo resolution.
9. Cards preserve the horror-comic visual bible.
10. The Hatchet Man is not approximated or substituted anywhere.
